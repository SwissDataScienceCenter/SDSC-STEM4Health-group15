# Bioreactor model shortlist for the fed-batch challenge

**Our problem:** predict VCD, TCD, substrate and byproduct for unseen batches from only the t=0 measurement plus the feed and volume profiles. Three of the four feeds have unknown composition, and interpretability is rewarded.

**Bottom line:** build #2 first, extend it into #1, and add #3 if time allows. Everything else is either a source of parts (#4–#12) or a poor fit for this data (second table).

Fit for us: ★★★★★ = build it, ★★★ = borrow parts, ★★ = ideas only.

## Suitable models, ranked by likely fit

| # | Model (key sources) | Executive summary | Works with only our data? | Evidence on unseen batches | Interpretability | Effort | Fit for us |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hybrid: mechanistic core + small neural net or Gaussian process correcting growth and lactate rates** (also called UDE / neural ODE). Bayer 2021, 2023; Pinto 2023; Richelle 2026 | Keeps exact mass balances and feed handling; a small network learns only what the kinetics get wrong. Matched or beat the purely mechanistic model in every CHO comparison found. Risks: overfitting with few batches, poor extrapolation beyond the feed range seen in training. | Yes | **Strong.** VCD error about 11–14% NRMSE on unseen runs (Bayer 2021). More accurate than mechanistic on all 4 data splits (Bayer 2023, 33 runs). | Medium. The learned correction can be plotted and turned back into formulas (#11). | Day 2, JAX or PyTorch | ★★★★★ **Likely best final model**, if it beats #2 with whole batches held out |
| 2 | **Lean mechanistic model:** Monod growth, glucose uptake from yield + maintenance, smooth lactate production-to-consumption switch, cell death + dead-cell lysis, hidden nutrient pool topped up by feeds B/C/D. About 10–15 parameters. | The foundation of #1 and the fallback if the hybrid overfits. Every parameter has a biological meaning, and the fitted value of each feed is process insight in its own right. Less accurate than hybrids, but less sensitive to which batches it is trained on. | Yes, designed for it | **Moderate.** Similar models on unseen runs: R² VCD 0.71, glucose 0.83, lactate 0.67, dead cells 0.96 (Okamura 2024). | **Highest** | Day 1, SciPy | ★★★★★ **Build first** |
| 3 | **Hierarchical (mixed-effects) version of #2 or #1:** 2–3 parameters (e.g. max growth rate, death rate) vary per batch around a shared mean. Helleckes 2022 | Gives honest uncertainty bands and tests whether t=0 values or feed totals explain differences between batches. No CHO paper has done this, so it is a novelty point for judges. It cannot learn a holdout batch's own quirks from t=0 alone. | Yes | Microbial only so far | High | Medium (NumPyro, or a simple penalty) | ★★★★ Strong add-on |
| 4 | **Sartorius CHO digital twin.** Richelle 2026; Kuchemüller 2026 | The closest published match to our task: 23 CHO runs with 8 feed formulations, and leave-one-batch-out accuracy was comparable to fitting all runs. Uses smooth switches, death driven by accumulated cell exposure, and a neural-net correction on growth. Lactate is still weak (R² 0.36). | Partly. Its dFBA layer needs metabolomics, so drop that layer. | **Strong** (leave-batch-out) | Medium–high | High to copy fully | ★★★★ **Template to copy from** |
| 5 | **Capacity term + hidden "limiting substrate."** Pörtner/Möller group; Kuchemüller 2023 | Growth stops via an unmeasured nutrient state, with no need to know which nutrient ran out, which is exactly our unknown-feed problem. The same group found an optimum with 4 runs where classical DoE needed 16–29. | Yes | Moderate | High | Low | ★★★★ Source of the nutrient-pool idea in #2 |
| 6 | **Multi Clone Kinetic Model (MCKM).** Sietaram 2025 | 13-parameter Monod model with a glucose-threshold lactate switch, fitted to 656 CHO runs. Showed max growth rate is stable across clones. Using one averaged feed schedule for all runs ruined its glucose fits (R² −0.24). Each run was fitted on its own, so there is no test on unseen batches. | Mostly (its death term uses ammonium) | **Weak** (in-sample only; lactate error 33–44%) | High | Low | ★★★ Parameter ranges and pitfalls |
| 7 | **High-density CHO model.** Okamura 2024 | Death from byproducts and starvation, with saturating lysis of dead cells. One of the few true held-out tests and the best realistic accuracy target. Its lactate switch uses glutamine and dissolved oxygen, neither of which we measure. | Partly | Good (one held-out run) | High | Low–medium | ★★★ Borrow its death and lysis terms |
| 8 | **Classic CHO fed-batch model.** Xing 2010; Kornecki & Strube 2018 | The textbook model: 6 ODEs and 18 parameters, with growth on glucose and glutamine and inhibition by lactate and ammonia. Lactate is tied to glucose by a fixed yield, so it cannot show the lactate shift. Needs glutamine and ammonia data. | Partly (drop the glutamine and ammonia terms) | Validated on 2 perturbed runs | High | Low | ★★★ Starting equations |
| 9 | **Lumped-network ("macroscopic") models.** Ben Yahia 2017, 2021; Zamorano 2013; Maton 2022 | Compresses the metabolic network into a few reactions between measured species. With 4 species that is about 3 reactions, essentially #2. Notable result: predicted new feeding strategies outside the training range, and scale-up from 2 L to 2000 L. | Yes, once reduced | Good (new feeding strategies) | High | Medium | ★★★ Justifies #2's structure; borrow the "growth slows with cumulative cell exposure" term |
| 10 | **Gaussian-process hybrid.** Doyle 2026; Hutter 2021 | Like #1, but a Gaussian process learns the rates. Uncertainty is built in, and it copes with very few batches. Beat a Monod model on intensified CHO; short-term effects right after each feed bolus remain unsolved. | Yes | Moderate (2 processes) | Medium | Medium | ★★★ Swap in for the neural net in #1 if data are scarce |
| 11 | **Equation discovery: symbolic regression, SINDy, "functional hybrids."** Narayanan 2022; Forster 2024 | Finds readable rate equations from data. Plain SINDy fails on saturating, switching kinetics and noisy daily samples; it works best on a trained hybrid's smooth rate curves. Extrapolated better than neural-net hybrids in one study. Never tested on CHO. | Yes | Simulated / microbial only | **Highest** | Medium | ★★★ Final step: turn #1's learned corrections into formulas |
| 12 | **Stochastic multi-scale model.** Wang, Harcum & Xie 2026 | Probabilistic model of growth-phase transitions: 1–5-day forecasts within 5.7–12% error, with well-calibrated 95% intervals, on 9 CHO runs. Code is public. Relies on online signals (oxygen uptake, pH) and forecasts from mid-run, not from t=0. | No | Good, but for short look-ahead only | Medium | High | ★★ Ideas for uncertainty only |

## Considered and ruled out for this data

| Model (key sources) | Why not |
|---|---|
| Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs). Yang 2024; Li 2026 | A basic PINN learns one trajectory; predicting new batches from t=0 and feeds needs extra machinery that #1 already handles. CHO results on unseen batches were behind paywalls. |
| Deep hybrid with many layers. Pinto 2023 | Its 24% gain came with 30 measured species. With our 4 species a deep network mainly adds overfitting risk, so keep #1's network small. |
| Small dynamic FBA models. Nolan & Lee 2011; Khare 2025 | 34–35 reactions and about 45 parameters, needing amino-acid data. Mispredicted a lactate-spike run. |
| Genome-scale dynamic FBA. Ghodba 2025; COSMIC-dFBA 2024; iCHO3K | 205–253 parameters and about 60 measured metabolites. Use only to sanity-check yield ranges. |
| Detailed kinetic-metabolic and cybernetic models. Robitaille 2015 (139 parameters); Martínez 2020; Mulukutla 2012–2015 | Need intracellular data and were never validated on independent runs. Useful only to explain why the lactate shift is partly unpredictable: identical conditions can end up in either of two metabolic states. |
| Ensemble Kalman filter adaptation. Yu & Kontoravdi 2026 | Updates the model using each new run's own measurements; our holdouts have only t=0. |
| Logistic / empirical growth curves. Craven 2013 | Cannot take feeds as input; failed on nutrients and byproducts in fed-batch. |
| Pure black box (gradient boosting, LSTM) | Not mechanistic and not interpretable. Build one anyway as a baseline to show the mechanistic structure adds value. |

Full citations and links are in `Mechanistic bioreactor metabolism models.md` in this folder.
