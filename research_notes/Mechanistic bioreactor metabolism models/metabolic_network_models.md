# Metabolic-network-informed dynamic models of CHO (mammalian) fed-batch metabolism: survey as of Nov 2026

Scope note: "Classic" = pre-2020; "cutting edge" = 2022–2026. Where a detail was not verifiable in a primary source during this session it is flagged or moved to Gaps. Application context used for the inferences: fed-batch, 10–14 d, many batches, measured VCD, TCD, glucose, lactate, volume; 4 bolus feeds (glucose + 3 of unknown composition); goal = predict unseen batches from t=0 + feed profile, interpretability valued.

## Q1. Macroscopic models from elementary flux modes (EFMs) / macro-reactions: how built, how many parameters, how they performed

### Takeaway
EFM-derived macroscopic models compress an intracellular network into a few "macro-reactions" linking only *measured* extracellular species, each given Monod/Michaelis-Menten-type kinetics. The key design rule in this line of work (Provost & Bastin → Zamorano → Maton 2022) is that the number of retained macro-reactions should be smaller than the number of measured extracellular species. With only glucose, lactate and biomass (+ dead cells) measured, that leaves about 2–3 macro-reactions. This is the most directly "reducible" network-based family for the dataset described. NOTE: the 2017 Hagrot "poly-pathway" J. Biotechnol. paper was **retracted**.

### Cited Findings
**Classic foundations (pre-2020)**
- Provost, Bastin, Agathos & Schneider (2006) set out a systematic method: treat the culture as a succession of phases, use one metabolic network per phase, and turn each into macroscopic bioreactions via EFMs. The phase models are then combined by "smoothly switching from model to model". Illustrated on batch CHO cultures. Bioprocess Biosyst Eng 29:349–366, doi:10.1007/s00449-006-0083-y — [Provost et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00449-006-0083-y)
- Zamorano, Vande Wouwer, Jungers & Bastin (2013): for a *detailed* CHO network, the number of EFMs explodes combinatorially and full enumeration becomes "impossible". They instead decompose admissible flux vectors into a *minimal number* of EFMs without enumerating them (Jungers et al. 2011 method). This gives macroscopic bioreactions linking the measured extracellular species at low computational cost. They repeat this per culture phase and build a global multi-model that "successfully predict[s] the evolution of experimental data". J Biotechnol 164:409–422, doi:10.1016/j.jbiotec.2012.05.005 — [Zamorano et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiotec.2012.05.005); thesis on CHO-320 cells — [Zamorano thesis](https://perso.uclouvain.be/georges.bastin/thesezamorano.pdf)
- Ben Yahia, Malphettes & Heinzle (2015) wrote a mini-review of macroscopic modeling strategies for mammalian cell growth and metabolism. Appl Microbiol Biotechnol, doi:10.1007/s00253-015-6743-6 — [Ben Yahia et al. 2015](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00253-015-6743-6.pdf)
- Ben Yahia, Gourevitch, Malphettes & Heinzle (2017), *segmented linear model*: specific metabolic rates are modelled as piecewise-linear functions of specific growth rate. Breakpoints ("metabolic shifts") are found by recursive partitioning plus hierarchical clustering. With structure and parameters from a 2 L training set, they "predict[ed] metabolic rates of new fed-batch cultures just using the experimental specific growth rates", both at 2 L and at 2000 L production scale. Biotechnol Bioeng 114:785–797, doi:10.1002/bit.26214 — [Ben Yahia et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.26214)
- Hagrot, Oddsdóttir, Hosta, Jacobsen & Chotteau (2017), "Poly-pathway model…amino acid depletion in CHO cell culture". Macro-reactions came from full EFM enumeration, with kinetics including saturation and inhibition by medium components. **One parameter set** was fitted across multiple metabolic states (amino-acid removal, reduction or doubling). J Biotechnol 259:235–247, doi:10.1016/j.jbiotec.2017.05.026 — [Hagrot et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiotec.2017.05.026). **This article was RETRACTED**: retraction notice J Biotechnol 265:127 (2018), doi:10.1016/j.jbiotec.2017.11.013 — [Retraction notice](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiotec.2017.11.013). The reason for the retraction was not retrieved.
- Hagrot, Oddsdóttir, Mäkinen, Forsgren & Chotteau (2019) replaced full enumeration with a **column-generation** optimization that selects the data-relevant EFMs. They applied it to a **126-reaction** CHO network under varied amino-acid availability and report "a good fit" of growth, product and uptake/secretion rate variations. Metab Eng Commun 8:e00083, doi:10.1016/j.mec.2018.e00083 — [Hagrot et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mec.2018.e00083). The column-generation method itself is in Oddsdóttir et al. — [arXiv 1410.2680](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.2680)
- Hagrot et al. also published "Dynamic metabolic network modeling of mammalian CHO cell cultures with continuous phase kinetics transitions" in Biochem Eng J, which extends the poly-pathway idea to cover all culture phases — [ScienceDirect record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1369703X18304170). A later citing paper credits "Kastelic et al. (2019) and Hagrot et al. (2017)" with modelling CHO metabolism across all culture phases — [search record](https://dc.engconfintl.org/cellculture_xv/124/)

**Cutting edge (2020–2026)**
- Maton, Bogaerts & Vande Wouwer (2022) give a systematic EFM-selection procedure. It starts from full enumeration or a subset, then reduces to a number of elementary flux vectors **below the number of measured extracellular components**. CHO batch cultures "can be efficiently represented by a few elementary flux vectors". J Process Control 118:170–184, doi:10.1016/j.jprocont.2022.09.002 — [Maton et al. 2022 (PDF)](https://dipot.ulb.ac.be/dspace/bitstream/2013/358040/3/1-s2.0-S0959152422001627-main.pdf)
- Martínez, Bulté, Contreras, Palomares & Ramírez (2020) built a hybrid cybernetic model (HCM) on EFMs (detail in Q3). The network has 89 reactions and gives 18,110,823 EMs unconstrained. Sequential constraints cut this to 7,855, and then "Polar Space Yield Analysis" picks a minimal active set. Front Bioeng Biotechnol 8:279, doi:10.3389/fbioe.2020.00279 — [Martínez et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7174696/)
- Ben Yahia, Malphettes & Heinzle (2021) combined piecewise-linear rate–growth relations with "mixed Monod-inhibition type kinetics…generalized to every possible external metabolite". µmax is a function of **integral viable cell density (IVCD)**. They also model accumulation of unmeasured intracellular metabolite pools that influence growth, "even without identification and quantification of these metabolites". The model predicted new, untested feeding strategies, "even outside the range of experimental data used for establishing the model". Metab Eng 66:204–216, doi:10.1016/j.ymben.2021.04.004 — [Ben Yahia et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymben.2021.04.004)
- Hybrid EFM + neural-network kinetics has a long history in mammalian bioprocesses. Elementary modes give the stoichiometry and a single-hidden-layer backprop NN learns the EM rates. Teixeira et al. 2007, BMC Bioinformatics 8:30 — [Hybrid elementary flux analysis/nonparametric modeling](https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2105-8-30)

### Inferences
- The EFM/macro-reaction approach is the only network-based family whose *output dimension is set by the measured species*, so it fits a 4-species dataset well. A plausible EFM-justified set for glucose/lactate/biomass would be: (R1) glucose (+ lumped nutrient N) → biomass + lactate (growth), (R2) glucose → lactate (overflow/maintenance), (R3) lactate (+N) → biomass/CO2 (post-switch lactate consumption). Each gets Monod/inhibition kinetics. That is about 2–4 parameters per reaction, so ~10–15 in total, similar to the 13-parameter MCKM in Q5.
- Ben Yahia 2021 is the closest classic-to-modern analogue to the target task: it predicted unseen feed strategies from extracellular data. Its two tricks map directly onto a "latent nutrient" model with unknown feeds: µmax(IVCD) as a proxy for an unmeasured inhibitor/depletion, and unmeasured intracellular pools.
- Do not cite Hagrot 2017 as evidence; use Hagrot 2019 (MEC) and the Biochem Eng J paper instead.

### Gaps
- No exact parameter counts were found for Zamorano 2013, Hagrot 2019 or Ben Yahia 2021; full texts were not accessible (ScienceDirect returned 403).
- No retraction reason was found for Hagrot 2017.
- No head-to-head benchmark was found comparing EFM-macroscopic models with unstructured Monod models on held-out fed-batch runs.

## Q2. Dynamic FBA (dFBA) and dynamic metabolic flux analysis (DMFA) for CHO, including genome-scale models

### Takeaway
CHO dFBA runs from small kinetic–stoichiometric hybrids (Nolan & Lee 2011: about 34 reactions and 24 metabolites, a dozen kinetic rate laws) to genome-scale dFBA (Ghodba 2025: 205–253 parameters, ~60 extracellular metabolites, 20 Ambr250 runs). All validated examples rely on rich extracellular data (amino acids, often NMR/LC-GC exo-metabolomics). None of the validated examples works from glucose/lactate/VCD alone. Genome-scale CHO models are mature (iCHO1766 → iCHO3K, 2026), but GEM-scale dynamic models remain heavily over-parameterized relative to 4 measured species.

### Cited Findings
**Classic (pre-2020)**
- Nolan & Lee (2011), "Dynamic model of CHO cell metabolism". A subset of intracellular reactions has kinetic rate expressions in *extracellular* concentrations plus temperature- and redox-dependent regulatory variables. Pseudo-steady-state flux distributions are solved at discrete times. Data from several fed-batch cultures were used to derive, fit and **validate** the model, which "accurately predicted the effects of process variables, including temperature shift, seed density, specific productivity, and nutrient concentrations". Metab Eng 13:108–124, doi:10.1016/j.ymben.2010.09.003 — [Nolan & Lee 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymben.2010.09.003)
- The Nolan & Lee network is described as "a minimal CHO network with 24 metabolites and 34 reactions" — [Ranpura et al. 2025 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12269988/). Nolan's thesis states that 12 intracellular cytosolic reactions get kinetic rate expressions — [Nolan PhD thesis, Tufts](https://dl.tufts.edu/concern/pdfs/m039kh11q). Follow-up: Nolan & Lee 2012, "Dynamic model for CHO cell engineering", J Biotechnol, doi:10.1016/j.jbiotec.2012.01.009 — [record](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/dynamic-model-for-cho-cell-engineering/813336485418762242-4191)
- Martínez, Dietmair, Quek, Hodson, Gray & Nielsen (2013) ran FBA (network derived from a mouse GEM) before and after the CHO lactate-production → consumption switch. Lactate-consuming cells had about **6× higher energy efficiency** (ATP per C-mol substrate). Lower TCA fluxes were around 60 µmol/gDW/h in both phases. Biotechnol Bioeng 110:660–666, doi:10.1002/bit.24728 — [Martínez et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.24728)
- Martínez et al. 2015 used B-spline-based DMFA to study the effect of temperature shift on CHO metabolism (cited as prior dynamic CHO flux work) — [Antonakoudis & Richelle 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.02.679947)
- DMFA "focuses on measuring changes in fluxes in cultures that are not at metabolic steady state" — [Ranpura et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12269988/)
- Hefzi et al. (2016), iCHO1766 consensus GEM: >1,700 genes, with cell-line-specific CHO-K1, CHO-S and CHO-DG44 models. It "accurately predict[s] growth phenotypes and known auxotrophies". Cell Systems 3:434–443, doi:10.1016/j.cels.2016.10.020 — [Hefzi et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2016.10.020). The review lists 1,766 genes and 3,229 reactions — [Ranpura et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12269988/)
- Calmels et al. (2019) applied a curated CHO-DG44 GEM to an industrial fed-batch process (601 reaction changes to iCHO1766) — [Calmels et al. 2019 record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/327677491_Application_of_a_curated_genome-scale_metabolic_model_of_CHO_DG44_to_an_industrial_fed-batch_process); [Ranpura et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12269988/)

**GEM lineage (2019–2026)**
- iCHO2291 (Yeo et al.) has 2,291 genes and 6,236 reactions; its enzyme-constrained FBA "significantly reduce[s] flux variability" and predicts lactate–pyruvate cycling. iCHO2101 (Fouladiha et al.) added 773 reactions and 335 genes by gap-filling. iCHO2441 (Strain et al., 2023) merges iCHO2291 with the secretory model iCHO2048s; cell-line-specific (GIMME/CORDA) versions predicted extracellular fluxes more accurately — [Ranpura et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12269988/); enzyme-capacity CHO GEM preprint — [Yeo et al. bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2019.12.19.883652.full.pdf)
- **iCHO3K** (community consensus; preprint Apr 2025, *Cell Systems* 2026, doi:10.1016/j.cels.2026.101574) has 11,004 reactions, 7,377 metabolites, 3,597 genes and 3,489 mapped protein structures. The distributed "unblocked" model has 8,370 reactions, 4,695 metabolites and 2,929 genes, in COBRApy/MATLAB/SBML/JSON. It was used to propose PEP allosteric inhibition of PFK in lactate-free (ZeLa) CHO — [iCHO3K GitHub](https://github.com/LewisLabUCSD/iCHO3K); [bioRxiv 2025.04.10.647063](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.10.647063.full.pdf)

**Cutting-edge dFBA (2023–2026)**
- **Ghodba, Richelle, McCready, Ricardez-Sandoval & Budman (2025)** built a CHO dFBA with kinetic constraints that depend on pH and temperature, validated on **20 Ambr250 fed-batch runs**. To "mitigate overparameterization", a BIC-based bi-level optimization pruned kinetic constraints and cut parameters **from 253 to 205**. Predictive accuracy improved by up to 8.3% (training) and 2.68% (validation). Average **R² ≥ 0.97** for growth and titer and **R² ≥ 0.85** for other metabolites, including amino-acid utilization. J Biotechnol 408:61–71, doi:10.1016/j.jbiotec.2025.08.010 — [Ghodba et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiotec.2025.08.010)
- Antonakoudis & Richelle (Sartorius, bioRxiv Oct 2025) proposed systematic data-driven GEM reduction for dynamic modelling. They took iCHO1766 (~6,663 reactions in the version used) down to **860 reactions / 623 metabolites / 725 genes**, and exchanges from >600 to 37. Data: 12 Ambr250 DG44 IgG1 fed-batches, 14 d, pH/temperature shifts at day 7, with **~60 extracellular metabolites** measured daily by NMR, FLEX and LC-GC (28 core metabolites used). Rates were estimated with Bayesian MetRaC under uncertainty. They report no held-out-run validation and no quantitative fit metric. They characterize Ghodba 2025's reduction as minimal (blocked reactions only) — [Antonakoudis & Richelle 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.02.679947)
- Khare, …, Kevrekidis, Harcum & Betenbaugh (bioRxiv Oct 2025) compared a "kinetic-oriented" and a "stoichiometric-oriented" hybrid dFBA. Network: 35 reactions and 24 metabolites (adapted from Nolan & Lee), with 14 kinetic rate laws and **45 kinetic parameters**. Data: 3 Ambr250 fed-batch conditions over 7 d, with VCD, glucose, lactate, Gln, Glu, NH4, IgG, amino acids and ¹³C labelling. They fit on the control condition and applied the model to a changed-initial-conditions run and a lactate-spiked run. The kinetic-oriented variant had lower error and captured the lactate and alanine production→consumption shifts "without a redox variable". Both variants mispredicted the 38 mM lactate-spike case. Code is available only on request — [Khare et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.28.685134)
- Golzarijalal et al. (SSRN preprint) built a dynamic genome-scale FBA framework for CHO fed-batch. It was fitted to a single 14-day run and used to generate 400,000 in-silico fed-batch runs — [SSRN 5591701](https://dx.doi.org/10.2139/ssrn.5591701)

**Tools/code**
- COBRApy (constraint-based modelling; actively maintained, last push Nov 2026) — [opencobra/cobrapy](https://github.com/opencobra/cobrapy)
- `dynamic-fba` (Tourigny) Python/C++ dFBA package — [gitlab.com/davidtourigny/dynamic-fba](https://gitlab.com/davidtourigny/dynamic-fba)
- iCHO3K model and notebooks — [LewisLabUCSD/iCHO3K](https://github.com/LewisLabUCSD/iCHO3K)

### Inferences
- Every validated CHO dFBA in this survey (Nolan & Lee 2011; Ghodba 2025; Khare 2025) used at least Gln/Glu/NH4 plus amino acids, and often tens of metabolites. With only glucose and lactate measured, an FBA solution space with hundreds of degrees of freedom (e.g., 210 DOF in Ramos 2022's reduced GEM, Q6) would be constrained by 2 exchange rates plus growth. Most fluxes, and the uptakes of the 3 unknown feeds, would be unidentifiable.
- A GEM still has a modest use here as a *prior*. It can bound stoichiometric yields (max lactate/glucose, ATP-consistent biomass yields) or justify the 2–3 macro-reactions in Q1. It should not be used as the dynamic engine.
- Ghodba 2025 found BIC-pruning only cut 253→205 parameters even with ~60 measured metabolites. That suggests the parameter burden of GEM-dFBA is structural and will not shrink to the scale that 4 species can support.

### Gaps
- No CHO dFBA was found that was trained and validated using only glucose/lactate/VCD.
- No details found on COSMIC-dFBA's held-out evaluation design (see Q6).
- The DOI for Martínez et al. 2015 (B-spline DMFA) was not retrieved; it is known only via citation.

## Q3. Kinetic-metabolic and cybernetic models of CHO; mechanistic explanations of the lactate shift

### Takeaway
Kinetic-metabolic models (Jolicoeur lab: Ghorbaniaghdam 2013/2014, Robitaille 2015) include energetic/redox regulation (ATP/ADP, NADH/NAD) but have about 70–140 parameters, of which only about 20 are fitted. They need intracellular metabolomics. Mechanistic studies (Mulukutla 2012/2014/2015, Hu lab) explain the lactate switch as glycolytic **bistability** (PFK/PK regulation) shifted by **lactate inhibition** and AKT/growth signalling. Hartley 2018 argues redox imbalance is the primary driver. For a 4-species model, these findings support a simple switch rule driven by glucose, lactate and growth rate, rather than a full kinetic model.

### Cited Findings
**Lactate-shift mechanism (classic → recent)**
- Mulukutla, Gramer & Hu (2012) found the shift to lactate consumption (NS0 fed-batch) "occurs upon the cessation of rapid growth and under conditions of low glycolysis flux and high extracellular lactate". Their kinetic model suggests high lactate inhibits PFK, lowers glycolytic flux and drives lactate → pyruvate, with AKT1 signalling playing "a key role". They conclude lactate consumption "is an outcome of reduced glycolysis flux, which is a product of lactate inhibition and regulatory action of signaling pathway caused by reduced growth rate". Metab Eng 14:138–149, doi:10.1016/j.ymben.2011.12.006 — [Mulukutla et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymben.2011.12.006)
- Mulukutla et al. (2014), glycolysis ODE model: bistability arises from PFK and PK regulation. Raising lactate from 0 to 20 mM increases the "switch-down" glucose concentration. PLoS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0098756 — [Mulukutla et al. 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=printable&id=10.1371/journal.pone.0098756)
- Mulukutla, Yongky, Grimm, Daoutidis & Hu (2015): lactate inhibition together with AKT regulation produces a complex steady-state topology. The high→low flux transition "can only occur in certain regions" of that topology, so the metabolic fate depends on the trajectory. This explains why the same cell line and medium sometimes shift and sometimes do not. Cultures that shifted had higher titers. PLoS ONE 10:e0121561, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0121561 — [Mulukutla et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0121561)
- Yongky et al. (2015) showed steady-state multiplicity in continuous culture caused by glycolytic bistability. Biotechnol Bioeng, doi:10.1002/bit.25566 — [Yongky et al. 2015](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/bit.25566)
- Hartley, Walker, Chung & Morten (2018) reviewed the lactate switch in CHO and argue **redox imbalance** is the primary driver. They also discuss pH, NAD+/NADH, pyruvate availability and mitochondrial function. Biotechnol Bioeng 115(8):1890–1903, doi:10.1002/bit.26603 — [Hartley et al. 2018](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:f713ef24-d559-4601-afb2-5177dca20e9b)
- Khare et al. (2025): a kinetic-oriented hybrid dFBA reproduced lactate and alanine production→consumption shifts *without* a redox state variable — [Khare et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.28.685134)

**Kinetic-metabolic (Jolicoeur lab, classic)**
- Ghorbaniaghdam, Henry & Jolicoeur (2013) built a kinetic-metabolic model based on cell energetic state. It uses Michaelis-Menten kinetics plus regulation by ATP/ADP, NAD/NADH and NADP/NADPH, and was applied to CHO under Na-butyrate. Bioprocess Biosyst Eng, doi:10.1007/s00449-012-0804-3 — [Ghorbaniaghdam et al. 2013](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00449-012-0804-3). A related thesis-based model is reported as 30 metabolic reactions with 72 parameters — [Ghorbaniaghdam thesis 2013](https://publications.polymtl.ca/1228/1/2013_AtefehGhorbaniaghdam.pdf) (count taken from a search summary; not checked in the PDF)
- Ghorbaniaghdam et al. (2014) used the model as an "in silico metabolomic platform" to analyse clonal variation in mAb-producing CHO, fitted to extra- *and intracellular* concentration data. PLoS ONE, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0090832 — [Ghorbaniaghdam et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3954614)
- Robitaille, Chen & Jolicoeur (2015) fitted a single kinetic-metabolic model (glycolysis, TCA, PPP, OxPhos, amino-acid metabolism) to 2 batch + 2 fed-batch CHO-DXB11 cultures on 2 media. It has **139 kinetic parameters, of which the 20 most sensitive** (Morris screening) were fitted. Data included amino acids (HPLC) and intracellular nucleotides, organic acids and sugar phosphates (UPLC-MS/MS). Only 3 parameters differed between media and none between batch and fed-batch. There was **no separate validation set**, and the model does not cover the decline phase. A shift to lactate *consumption* was not clearly observed. PLoS ONE 10:e0136815, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0136815 — [Robitaille et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0136815)

**Cybernetic (Ramkrishna-style hybrid cybernetic models)**
- Martínez et al. (2020) built a hybrid cybernetic model on EFMs for CHO-S. Each EM has a Michaelis-Menten rate (k, K), and cybernetic variables regulate enzyme synthesis and activity via matching laws. Two EM families were used (glucose-dominant, and one from maximum lactate consumption around 44–92 h). Parameters were fitted by a genetic algorithm; the total count is not stated. Data: triplicate **batch** shake flasks, 144 h, with only viable/dead cells, glucose, lactate, Gln and Glu measured. The model **captures the lactate production→consumption shift** with MPPE < 10% (best around 1.4%), but **no independent validation** was done. MATLAB code: [github.com/JuanAndresMtz/PSYA](https://github.com/JuanAndresMtz/PSYA) — [Martínez et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7174696/)

**Recent multi-cell-line mechanistic models**
- Lu, McCann, Hu & Zhang (2024) built mechanistic metabolic models that integrate growth-signalling control of enzyme activity with transcript dynamics, for 3 CHO cell lines, using fed-batch data plus time-series transcriptomics. "Multi-cell-line learning" trains the cell-line models jointly to improve accuracy "with a limited amount of data" and gives high accuracy in predicting distinct behaviours. Biotechnol Bioeng 121:2833–2847, doi:10.1002/bit.28757 — [Lu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.28757)

### Inferences
- The Mulukutla/Hu results support encoding the lactate switch in a low-dimensional model as a **state-dependent switch** with these triggers: low glucose (or low glycolytic flux), high lactate, and falling growth rate. One example is a smooth sigmoid in glucose and lactate that turns on a lactate-uptake macro-reaction. The trajectory-dependence they describe (bistability) is a warning: batches with similar inputs may diverge in lactate. A deterministic model fed only t=0 and feeds may therefore have irreducible error on lactate.
- Full kinetic-metabolic models (Robitaille: 139 parameters, intracellular metabolomics) are clearly over-parameterized for the target dataset. Only their *qualitative* regulatory structure is transferable.
- Lu 2024's joint learning across cell lines is a useful template for "many batches, few species": shared mechanistic parameters plus a small number of batch- or clone-specific ones.

### Gaps
- No total parameter count was found for Martínez 2020's HCM or for Lu 2024's models.
- No Ramkrishna-lab cybernetic model applied specifically to CHO *fed-batch* with held-out validation was found in this session.

## Q4. Multi-scale models (cell cycle, population balance, glycosylation), briefly

### Takeaway
Multi-scale CHO models couple metabolism to glycosylation (Kontoravdi/Jiménez del Val lineage) or to population heterogeneity and phase transitions (Xie/Harcum 2024–2025). They target product quality or stochastic variability and need glycan, omics or online data. They are not suitable for a 4-species trajectory-prediction task, except as inspiration for phase/state-mixture structure.

### Cited Findings
- Glycosylation network models: Krambeck & Betenbaugh (2005) had 22,800 reactions, 11 enzymes and 7,565 N-glycans. Kotidis et al.'s CHOGlycoNET has 597 reactions and 326 oligosaccharides, built from 200 glycan datasets. Kotidis & Kontoravdi used a shallow ANN (hidden layers of 22 and 18 neurons) that outperformed linear models. Sha et al. used a two-step stoichiometric–kinetic model for glycoform prediction — [Ranpura et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12269988/)
- Kyriakopoulos et al. (2018) note that the "unstructured-unsegregated approach utilizing empirical Monod-type kinetics" dominates mammalian bioprocess kinetic modelling "due to its traceability and simple formalism". Large-scale kinetic models are "severely hampered by the scarcity of kinetic data and limitations in current parameter estimation methods". They see multi-scale modelling as the way forward. Biotechnol J 13:e1700229, doi:10.1002/biot.201700229 — [Kyriakopoulos et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/biot.201700229)
- Wang, Harcum & Xie (arXiv 2024, rev. Nov 2025) combine a stochastic single-cell metabolic network model, a probabilistic phase-transition model and a macro-kinetic model of heterogeneous populations. They claim accurate long-term CHO forecasts from online OUR/pH plus initial conditions; quantitative metrics were not available on the abstract page — [Wang et al. arXiv 2412.03883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03883)

### Inferences
- The one transferable idea is a **population mixture of metabolic states**: growth-state vs production/lactate-consuming-state cells, with a transition rate. This appears in Wang/Harcum/Xie and in COSMIC-dFBA (Q6). With VCD and TCD measured, a 2-state viable population plus a dead/lysed compartment is identifiable and interpretable.

### Gaps
- Primary glycosylation-coupling papers (Jiménez del Val et al.; Kotidis et al. 2019) were not fetched in this session; details above come from the 2025 review.

## Q5. Per-approach comparison: states/parameters, data needs, validation on unseen fed-batch runs, and reducibility to "glucose/lactate/biomass + latent nutrient"

### Takeaway
Size of measured data is the deciding factor. Every network-based CHO model in this survey that was validated on new fed-batch conditions used amino acids and/or exo-metabolomics (≥7 species, often ~30–60). Models built on only VCD/glucose/lactate-scale data are low-dimensional (≈10–15 parameters), e.g. MCKM (Sietaram 2025), Ben Yahia, and EFM macro-models with 2–3 macro-reactions. For the target dataset, network models are over-parameterized as dynamic engines. They are worth using only as structure priors (macro-reaction stoichiometry, yield bounds, switch logic).

- Sietaram, Kotidis, Finka & Lapkin (2025) published a Multi Clone Kinetic Model (MCKM). It has 7 ODEs (VCD, glucose, Gln, Glu, mAb, NH4, lactate) plus a µ state and **13 parameters**, with Monod growth on glucose and ammonium-dependent death. The **lactate switch is a glucose threshold** set from the average glucose at switch onset across runs. Bolus feeds are handled by integrating between samples and adding feed. Data: 656 fed-batch runs of 157 clones in Ambr15, about 15 d, 7 samples per run, no amino acids beyond Gln/Glu. Each run was fitted independently (no parameter sharing). Biomass R² was about 0.96 ± 0.07 and NRMSE 6–7%, but **lactate NRMSE was ~33–44%** and glucose R² was negative for one campaign. Some yields were collinear (e.g., Y_lac/glc with Y_X/lac). The authors note conventional kinetic models need many cultures per regression, whereas MCKM can be fitted from one run with 49 data points. J Ind Microbiol Biotechnol 52:kuaf029, doi:10.1093/jimb/kuaf029 — [Sietaram et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12486387/)
- The 2025 review states that for kinetic models "the number of parameters estimated can become very large, while the data available to describe them is limited". Stoichiometric models are underdetermined with "insufficient experimental measurements to plug the gap". Validation should use independent datasets, plus profile likelihood, sensitivity and variance-based identifiability analysis — [Ranpura et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12269988/)
- Maton et al. (2022) reduce the number of elementary flux vectors to below the number of measured components, so the macro-model stays identifiable from extracellular data — [Maton et al. 2022](https://dipot.ulb.ac.be/dspace/bitstream/2013/358040/3/1-s2.0-S0959152422001627-main.pdf)
- Ben Yahia et al. (2021) captured unmeasured intracellular pool effects and growth slowdown via µmax(IVCD) and still predicted untested feed strategies — [Ben Yahia et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymben.2021.04.004)

### Inferences
- **Are network-based models worth it here?** Not as full dFBA, kinetic-metabolic or GEM models. With 4 measured species and 3 feeds of unknown composition, the identifiable information is about: growth/death kinetics (VCD and TCD give death and lysis), glucose uptake, lactate production and consumption, and one or two latent limitation effects. That supports about 10–20 parameters shared across batches. GEM dFBA (≥200 parameters, ~60 metabolites) and Robitaille-type models (139 parameters, intracellular metabolomics) would be badly unidentifiable.
- **Recommended reduction** (an inference, not an established published model for this exact data). An EFM-justified macroscopic model with:
  - States: X_v, X_d (from TCD − VCD), Glc, Lac, V, plus a latent nutrient N (or a latent inhibitor/"age" variable such as IVCD, following Ben Yahia 2021).
  - 2–3 macro-reactions: growth (Glc + N → X + Lac), overflow/maintenance (Glc → Lac), and lactate consumption (Lac → CO2/biomass), the last switched on by low glucose, high lactate or low µ per Mulukutla.
  - Unknown feed compositions treated as per-feed scalar contributions to N (3 parameters shared across batches).
  - This keeps interpretability (yields, Km, switch threshold) and mirrors MCKM-scale parameter counts.
- MCKM's poor lactate NRMSE (33–44%) with a glucose-threshold switch suggests lactate is the hardest species even for well-tuned kinetic models. Consider a smooth glucose × lactate switch, a hybrid ML correction on the lactate rate only, or a 2-subpopulation formulation.
- Pooling batches (as in Lu 2024's multi-cell-line learning) with shared mechanistic parameters plus few batch-specific random effects is better suited to "many batches, unseen-batch prediction" than per-run fitting (MCKM).

### Gaps
- No published CHO study was found that explicitly lumps a network model down to "glucose/lactate/biomass + latent nutrient" and validates it on held-out fed-batch runs. This is the most relevant missing benchmark.
- Parameter counts for Zamorano 2013, Hagrot 2019, Ben Yahia 2021 and Nolan & Lee 2011 were not retrievable (paywalled full texts).

## Q6. Most recent (2023–2026) work: hybrid dFBA + ML, GEM-derived kinetic models, reduced EFM models with ML kinetics, benchmarks

### Takeaway
The 2023–2026 frontier has four strands: (1) ML-augmented dFBA (COSMIC-dFBA 2024), (2) hybrid semi-parametric FBA (Ramos/Oliveira 2022) and NN-constrained FBA (NEXT-FBA), (3) BIC-pruned or data-reduced GEM dFBA (Ghodba 2025; Antonakoudis & Richelle 2025), and (4) kinetic- vs stoichiometric-priority hybrids (Khare 2025). All rely on rich exo-metabolomics. None was found benchmarked against simple Monod/hybrid-ML baselines on held-out runs with only glucose/lactate/VCD.

### Cited Findings
- **COSMIC-dFBA** (Gopalakrishnan, Johnson, …, Kontoravdi & Lewis; Metab Eng 82:183–192, 2024, doi:10.1016/j.ymben.2024.02.012). ML decomposes instantaneous uptake and secretion rates into weighted contributions from **cell states** (growth vs antibody-producing) and couples them with a GEM. It "can be parameterized with only metabolite concentrations from spent media". It predicts final cell density and titer "to within 10%", and gives 90% (cell density) and 72% (titer) improvement over standard dFBA — [Gopalakrishnan et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymben.2024.02.012); preprint [bioRxiv 2023.09.13.557646](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.09.13.557646.full.pdf)
- **HybridFBA** (Ramos, Oliveira, Dumas & Oliveira; Bioprocess Biosyst Eng 45:1889–1904, 2022, doi:10.1007/s00449-022-02795-9) adds mechanistic constraints from a reduced CHO-K1 GEM (686 metabolites, 788 reactions, **210 degrees of freedom**) and non-parametric constraints from PCA of the flux dataset in one LP. Data: 27 measured exchange fluxes from 21 reactor experiments. It significantly improves growth-rate prediction and was used to design a low-byproduct feed — [Ramos et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00449-022-02795-9)
- The 2025 review also covers NEXT-FBA (ANN-derived constraints for CHO GEMs, Morrissey and coworkers); Cui et al.'s gray-box neural ODE with a differentiable convex-optimization (FBA-like) layer; and Schinn et al. coupling GEMs with statistical learning to estimate amino-acid concentrations. Years are not given in the visible text — [Ranpura et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12269988/)
- Ghodba et al. 2025 (BIC-pruned pH/T dFBA, 20 runs, validation R²) — see Q2 — [Ghodba et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiotec.2025.08.010)
- Antonakoudis & Richelle 2025 (uncertainty-aware GEM reduction to ~860 reactions from 12 fed-batches with ~60 metabolites) — see Q2 — [bioRxiv 2025.10.02.679947](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.02.679947)
- Khare et al. 2025 (45-parameter kinetic-priority hybrid dFBA beats stoichiometric-priority; fails to extrapolate to 38 mM lactate spike) — see Q2 — [bioRxiv 2025.10.28.685134](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.10.28.685134)
- Lu et al. 2024 (multi-cell-line joint learning of mechanistic models with transcript dynamics) — see Q3 — [Lu et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1002/bit.28757)
- Wang, Harcum & Xie 2024/2025 (multi-scale hybrid with metabolic phase transitions, online-data-driven forecasting) — see Q4 — [arXiv 2412.03883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.03883)
- iCHO3K (2025 preprint / 2026 Cell Systems), the largest consensus CHO GEM, with code — [GitHub](https://github.com/LewisLabUCSD/iCHO3K)
- Deep hybrid bioreactor models: general first-principles + deep FFNN (ReLU, dropout, ADAM) framework from the Oliveira group — [bioRxiv 2022.06.07.495118](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.07.495118.full.pdf)

### Inferences
- The COSMIC-dFBA "cell-state mixture" idea can be carried into a 4-species setting without a GEM: two macro-reaction rate vectors (growth-state vs production/lactate-consuming state), with an ML or sigmoid weight driven by observable features (glucose, lactate, IVCD, time since feed). This keeps interpretability.
- Hybrid semi-parametric modelling (Oliveira group) is the strongest documented route for "mechanistic mass balances + learned kinetics". For this dataset the mechanistic part would be the volume-aware fed-batch mass balances plus EFM-style macro-stoichiometry. The ML part would learn specific rates (µ, q_glc, q_lac) from the state.
- No 2023–2026 benchmark was found that compares network-based dynamic models against unstructured Monod or hybrid-ML models on the *same* held-out CHO fed-batch runs. Claims of superiority (e.g., COSMIC vs standard dFBA) are made against other network models, not against simple baselines.
